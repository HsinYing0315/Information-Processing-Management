\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

% MR museums and AI NPCs
As mixed reality (MR) technologies become more common in museum settings, AI-driven non-player characters (AI NPCs) offer new possibilities for guiding visitors through interactive, situated, and socially engaging cultural experiences. Rather than a single settled definition, MR is best understood as a spectrum of experiences that combine physical and virtual content along several dimensions—among them immersion, virtuality, and interaction~\citep{speicherWhatMixedReality2019}. Within cultural heritage, MR has been used to move the museum beyond the mere display of information toward emotive, immersive, and richly interactive encounters with artifacts, for example through head-worn displays that overlay computer-generated content onto real exhibits~\citep{hammadyFrameworkConstructingEvaluating2021, marti-testonMethodologyProduceAugmentedReality2021}. Empirical work further suggests that wearable MR systems can improve visitors' retention, comprehension, and situational interest relative to conventional mobile guides~\citep{chinApplyingWearableMRbased2024}. In parallel, AI-driven agents such as chatbots have been introduced into museum learning to provide adaptive, on-demand feedback and to sustain visitors' cognitive and emotional engagement~\citep{liangEffectAIbasedChatbot2024}. Bringing these threads together, AI-driven virtual guides can be embodied as conversational NPCs in MR environments, allowing visitors to interact with them while engaging with physical exhibits~\citep{hammadyFrameworkConstructingEvaluating2021, yuIntegratingMixedReality}.

% Personality as a social design mechanism
Beyond providing information, AI NPCs can be designed with distinct personality traits that shape how users perceive and interact with them as social partners. The Big Five model, particularly extraversion, has been widely used to characterize and manipulate personality in artificial agents~\citep{neffEvaluatingEffectGesture2010, ahmadFrameworkPersonalityCues2022}. Prior research suggests that agent personality can influence perceived humanness, social presence, communication satisfaction, and user engagement, with extraverted agents often eliciting more positive social responses~\citep{leeCanRobotsManifest2006, ahmadCommunicatingMachinesConversational2021, volkelUserPerceptionsExtraversion2022}. More recent work further demonstrates that personality traits can be expressed across different forms of artificial agents, including large language model-based conversational systems~\citep{jiangPersonaLLMInvestigatingAbility2024}. Together, these findings establish personality as an important social design mechanism in human--agent interaction.

% From human--AI interaction to human--human--AI interaction
Although prior work has often examined AI agents in one-to-one human--AI interaction, museum visits frequently occur in social settings where visitors experience exhibits together with companions. \citet{tolmieSupportingGroupInteractions2014} show through ethnographic study that visiting is fundamentally a social activity: group members continually coordinate their movement and attention, and engagement with an exhibit often ends prematurely because of the need to keep up with, summon, or attend to fellow visitors. Maintaining group coherence while still allowing individual engagement is therefore a central design challenge, and prior systems have addressed it by reconfiguring the social dynamics of the visit rather than directing individuals in isolation~\citep{foshSupportingGroupCoherence2016, foshGiftingPersonalInterpretations2014}. Yet most personality-focused agent research still evaluates individually framed human--agent exchanges, giving comparatively little attention to how an agent's social behavior plays out when several co-present people share the same agent~\citep{ahmadCommunicatingMachinesConversational2021, volkelUserPerceptionsExtraversion2022}. This leaves open how personality cues operate in socially situated encounters, where social presence and interaction quality depend not only on the agent's behavior but also on the relationship and coordination between the visitors. Moving from human--AI to human--human--AI interaction therefore raises new questions about how an AI NPC's personality shapes not only each visitor's experience but also the social dynamics between companions~\citep{ohSystematicReviewSocial2018, yuIntegratingMixedReality}.

% Research Objective
Taken together, prior research suggests that personality is an important social design mechanism for shaping how users perceive and interact with AI agents. However, most existing studies have examined personality effects in dyadic human-AI settings, while museum visits are often socially situated experiences involving companions who jointly attend to, interpret, and coordinate around exhibits. This creates an important gap in understanding how an AI NPC's personality functions not only as a feature of individual human-AI interaction, but also as a factor that may shape collaborative engagement, visual attention, and interpersonal dynamics within visitor pairs.

To address this gap, this research investigates how AI NPC personality influences users' perceptions and behaviors in MR museum experiences across both individual and dyadic interaction contexts. In particular, it examines personality-related differences in users' evaluations, visual-attention behavior, and collaborative engagement, and further investigates how these patterns are associated with user--NPC personality similarity and vary across pair-acquaintance conditions.

To guide this investigation, we formulated five research questions addressing AI NPC personality effects, personality similarity, dyadic collaboration, and pair acquaintance as follow: 

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1.} How does AI NPC personality influence users’ perceptions and interaction behaviors in an individual MR museum experience?
    \item \textbf{RQ2.} To what extent does personality similarity between users and AI NPCs shape users’ evaluations of the NPC?
    \item \textbf{RQ3.} How does AI NPC personality influence users' subjective evaluations and visual-attention behavior in a collaborative dyadic MR experience? 
    \item \textbf{RQ4.} How do personality-related responses to AI NPCs differ between acquainted and unacquainted pairs?
    \item \textbf{RQ5.} To what extent does personality similarity between users and AI NPCs shape users' evaluations and visual-attention behavior across pair acquaintance conditions?
\end{itemize}

% Overview of the study
To address these research questions, we conducted a sequential multi-study investigation that progressively extended the interaction context from individual human--AI interaction to collaborative human--human--AI interaction. The research program consisted of an initial personality-manipulation study, an individual MR experiment, a dyadic pilot study, and a formal dyadic experiment. Across these studies, the personality manipulation was first validated, its effects were then examined in individual interaction, and the interaction context was subsequently extended to dyads to investigate whether these effects persisted or changed in the presence of another human partner.

The first study validated whether the designed verbal and vocal cues produced perceptually distinguishable introverted and extroverted AI NPCs. Building on this validation, the single-user experiment examined the effects of NPC personality on users' subjective evaluations, interaction behavior, and visual attention, as well as the role of user--NPC personality similarity (RQ1--RQ2). We then introduced a second human participant through a dyadic pilot study, which was used to identify interaction, task-design, and personality-manipulation issues arising in a shared MR setting and to refine the system before formal evaluation. The subsequent dyadic experiment employed a mixed design in which NPC personality was manipulated within pairs and pair acquaintance was varied between acquainted and unacquainted pairs. This experiment examined personality effects in collaborative interaction, the role of pair acquaintance, and user--NPC personality similarity across social contexts (RQ3--RQ5).

Finally, we conducted cross-study analyses to compare personality-related responses across individual, acquainted-dyad, and unacquainted-dyad conditions. This comparison allowed us to examine not only whether AI NPC personality affected user responses within each interaction context, but also whether the magnitude and pattern of these effects changed as the AI NPC moved from being the primary social partner in individual interaction to one participant within a broader human--human--AI social configuration.

\section{Pilot Study: Design and Manipulation Check of AI NPC Personality}

Building on the role of personality as a social design mechanism in human-AI interaction, this study first required a reliable manipulation of AI NPC personality before examining its effects in MR museum experiences. In particular, because the formal single-user experiment investigates how introverted and extroverted NPCs influence users' perceptions and interaction behaviors, it was essential to verify that these personality traits could be perceived as intended.

We therefore developed an AI-based mixed reality (AIMR) museum guide system on the Microsoft HoloLens. The system featured historical non-player characters (NPCs) driven by GPT-4 and the Azure text-to-speech (TTS) service, with two personality conditions: introverted and extroverted. The NPCs' personalities were conveyed through their generated language and synthesized voice. A pilot study was conducted prior to the formal experiment to assess the effectiveness of the personality manipulation and to confirm that users could appropriately recognize the intended introverted and extroverted traits.


The present study develops an AI-based mixed reality (AIMR) guide system on the Microsoft HoloLens, in which historical non-player characters (NPCs) driven by GPT-4 and the Azure text-to-speech (TTS) service are designed to exhibit introverted and extroverted personalities. As the personality of an AI NPC is conveyed simultaneously through its generated language and its synthesized voice, a pilot study was conducted prior to the formal lab experiments to verify whether the designed personality traits can be appropriately perceived by users.

NPC personality was manipulated through two production channels that jointly generated each AI NPC response: a \textbf{verbal channel}, in which GPT-4 generated the response text based on the system prompt, and a \textbf{vocal channel}, in which Azure TTS rendered the generated text as speech. Table 1 summarizes the cues used to differentiate the introverted and extroverted NPCs across these two channels. The cue selection was informed by prior research on linguistic expression, social interaction, and personality-related communication.

For the verbal channel, personality cues were specified in the GPT-4 system prompt and the manipulation covered several aspects of generated language. In general, the introverted NPC was prompted to use more neutral, precise, formal, cautious, and reserved language with fewer social expressions and shorter responses. In contrast, the extroverted NPC was prompted to use more socially expressive, direct, positive, and energetic language with more engagement markers and longer responses. Because these cues were implemented through the system prompt, their realization in individual GPT-4 responses was probabilistic rather than fixed.

For the vocal channel, personality differences were implemented directly through Azure TTS synthesis parameters. The introverted NPC used a lower speech rate and volume together with the “serious” speaking-style preset, whereas the extroverted NPC used a higher speech rate and volume together with the “cheerful” preset. Unlike the verbal cues generated by GPT-4, these vocal parameters were explicitly specified for each condition and therefore remained consistent across interactions. The verbal and vocal manipulations were designed to provide mutually consistent personality signals across modalities.

\subsection{Experimental Procedure for the Pilot Study}

\subsubsection{Pilot Test Designs}

Extroversion is better understood as a continuous spectrum rather than a dichotomous trait, where the distinction between introversion and extroversion is relative rather than absolute. Therefore, the manipulation check measured the perceived degree of extroversion of each AI NPC rather than relying on a categorical judgment. This approach allows the two AI NPCs to be compared along the same personality dimension and quantifies the extent of the designed personality differences before examining their effects in the subsequent laboratory experiment.

Two videos were used as the experimental stimuli, featuring an introverted and an extroverted AI NPC. Each NPC introduced ancient Chinese weaponry exhibits using the corresponding linguistic and vocal features designed for its personality condition.

To evaluate the effects of the text prompt and the vocal parameter adjustments on the perceived personality traits of the AI NPCs, an online questionnaire experiment was conducted using a within-subject design, in which a total of 20 participants were recruited. Each participant watched both videos and rated the perceived extroversion of each AI NPC using six items drawn from the extraversion domain of the Big Five Inventory-2 (BFI-2; \cite{sotoNextBigFive2017}). Extroversion was represented by three facets: sociability, assertiveness, and energy level, with two items measuring each facet. All items were rated on a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree).

Because the original BFI-2 was designed to assess human personality, the item wording was adapted to the AI NPC context while preserving the meaning of the original measures. Example items include ``\textit{This AI NPC rarely feels excited}'' and ``\textit{This AI NPC is full of energy}.''

\subsubsection{Results of the Pilot Test}

The results showed that the introverted AI NPC received a lower overall extroversion rating ($M = 2.775$, $SD = 0.928$) than the extroverted AI NPC ($M = 4.242$, $SD = 0.409$). Table~\ref{tab:manipulation_check} summarizes participants' ratings of sociability, assertiveness, and energy level for the two AI NPCs, where the extroverted NPC consistently received higher ratings across all three facets. Wilcoxon signed-rank tests further revealed significant differences in all three facets of extroversion. Specifically, compared with the introverted NPC, the extroverted AI NPC was rated as more sociable ($M = 3.750$ vs.\ $2.775$, $W = 4.0$, $p < .001$), more assertive ($M = 4.488$ vs.\ $3.013$, $W = 7.0$, $p < .001$), and more energetic ($M = 4.488$ vs.\ $2.538$, $W = 4.0$, $p < .001$).

These results indicate that the combination of LLM prompting and TTS adjustments successfully produced distinguishable introverted and extroverted AI NPCs. The significant differences across sociability, assertiveness, and energy level confirm the effectiveness of the personality manipulation. Our findings are consistent with \citet{serapio-garciaPersonalityTraitsLarge2023}, who demonstrated that extroversion can be manipulated through LLM-generated text, and further extend this approach by jointly manipulating the linguistic and vocal features of AI NPCs.

\section{Lab Experiment: Effects of AI NPC Personality in Single-User Interaction}\label{sec:lab}

Following the validation of the AI NPC personality manipulation in the pilot study, the first lab experiment examined how introverted and extroverted AI NPCs influenced users' experiences during actual use of the MR museum guide system. Using a within-subjects design, participants interacted with both personality conditions, allowing us to examine differences in subjective evaluations, interaction behaviors, and visual attention, as well as whether these effects varied according to user--NPC personality similarity.

\subsection{Participants}

Participants’ extraversion was assessed using the Extraversion domain of the BFI-2 questionnaire. The scale consists of 12 items rated on a five-point Likert scale, yielding a possible summed score ranging from 12 to 60. A total score of 36 represents the theoretical midpoint of the scale, corresponding to an average item response of 3. Based on this midpoint, participants scoring 36 or below were classified as introverts, whereas those scoring above 36 were classified as extroverts. A total of 16 participants aged between 20 and 30 years were recruited, comprising eight participants in each group.

\subsection{Experimental Design and Procedure}

The study adopted a within-subject design in which each participant interacted with both an introverted and an extroverted AI NPC. To control for potential order effects, participants were randomly assigned to one of two interaction orders.

Before the experiment, participants received an overview of the study procedure and reviewed several example questions that could be asked during the interaction. They then wore a Microsoft HoloLens 2 MR headset and interacted with each AI NPC for up to eight minutes. Each interaction began with a self-introduction by the NPC, after which participants were provided with two predefined questions to initiate the conversation.

After completing both interaction sessions, participants undertook a knowledge task to assess whether they had sufficiently engaged with the exhibit content. Participants who scored below 80\% were excluded from the analysis. They subsequently completed a post-experiment questionnaire and participated in a short semi-structured interview. The entire study lasted approximately 35-40 minutes.

\subsection{Measures}

\subsubsection{Subjective Measures}

To capture participants' subjective perceptions and evaluations of the AI NPCs, post-interaction questionnaires were administered using a five-point Likert scale. The measured constructs included social presence (SP), continuance intention to use (ITU), and satisfaction (SAT), which were used to address the perceptual dimension of RQ1. Participants' extraversion, assessed using the BFI-2 before the experiment, was additionally used in the similarity-attraction analysis for RQ2.

Social presence was measured using five items adapted from~\cite{gefenConsumerTrustB2C2004}, with satisfactory reliability and convergent validity reported in the original scale ($\mathrm{CR}=0.919$, $\sqrt{\mathrm{AVE}}=0.836$). Continuance intention to use was measured using items adapted from~\cite{liChatbotsEcommerceEffect2023} ($\alpha=0.93$, $\mathrm{CR}=0.94$, $\mathrm{AVE}=0.78$), and satisfaction was assessed using items adapted from Shi et al. ($\mathrm{CR}=0.910$, $\mathrm{AVE}=0.717$).

\subsubsection{Behavioral Measures}

Behavioral measures were collected through system logs, direct observation, and eye-tracking recordings to address the interaction-behavior dimension of RQ1. Interaction frequency was operationalized as the number of participant-NPC interactions, including verbal questions and selections of predefined prompts. Visual attention was assessed using fixation counts and durations across three predefined Areas of Interest (AOIs): the AI NPC, the physical exhibit, and the virtual exhibit. These measures were used to derive total gaze duration, fixation frequency, and the distribution of visual attention across AOIs.

\subsection{Results}

The results are organized around the two research questions. RQ1 was examined using participants' subjective evaluations, interaction frequency, and eye-tracking measures, whereas RQ2 was addressed through the similarity–attraction analysis based on participant personality and responses to the two AI NPCs.

\subsubsection{Questionnaire (Subjective Evaluation)}

To address RQ1, we examined whether NPC personality influenced participants' subjective evaluations of social presence (SP), intention to use (ITU), and satisfaction (SAT). As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:single-rate}, ratings were generally higher for the extroverted NPC across all three constructs, with the largest difference observed for SP. Shapiro-Wilk tests indicated that the difference scores for ITU and SAT deviated from normality ($p<.05$), while SP did not ($p=.0942$). Given the small sample size ($N=16$) and the non-normality observed for multiple constructs, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests were used for all within-subject comparisons. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:single_user_results}, ratings for the extroverted NPC were significantly higher than those for the introverted NPC across SP ($W=0.0$, $p<.001$, $r=.68$), ITU ($W=9.5$, $p=.007$, $r=.63$), and SAT ($W=0.0$, $p=.015$, $r=.70$). Because tied difference scores were present in more than 10\% of the observations, sign tests and paired permutation tests were additionally conducted as robustness checks, yielding the same overall pattern of significance. Taken together, the results show significant differences between the two NPC personality conditions across SP, ITU, and SAT, with higher ratings observed for the extroverted AI NPC.

\subsubsection{Similarity Attraction}

To address RQ2 and assess whether personality similarity influenced participants’ evaluations of the AI NPCs, Mann–Whitney U tests were conducted across participant personality groups. Extroverted participants consistently showed positive similarity scores, indicating a preference for the personality-matched extroverted AI across social presence, intention to use, and satisfaction. In contrast, introverted participants showed negative similarity scores, suggesting either a preference for the dissimilar extroverted AI or no clear similarity-based preference. The preference for the extroverted AI was significantly stronger among extroverted participants than among introverted participants across all constructs ($p < .05$; Table~\ref{tab:attraction_test}). These findings therefore provide partial evidence for a similarity-attraction pattern, with the similarity–attraction effect emerging primarily among extroverted participants.

Consistent with these subjective evaluations, the eye-tracking results provide behavioral, yet conditional, support for the similarity–attraction hypothesis. Extroverted participants showed positive mean differences in gaze count (+35.88) and gaze duration (+11.86 ms), indicating greater visual attention toward the personality-matched extroverted AI. In contrast, introverted participants exhibited negative differences, directing more visual attention toward the personality-dissimilar extroverted AI. Mann–Whitney U tests further revealed significant between-group differences in both gaze duration ($U = 11.0$, $p < .05$, $r = .55$) and gaze count ($U = 9.0$, $p < .05$, $r = .60$). Taken together, these findings suggest that participant personality significantly shaped gaze allocation, while the similarity–attraction effect was observed more consistently among extroverted than introverted participants.

\subsection{Discussion}

The single-user study showed a clear advantage of the extroverted AI NPC in subjective evaluations. Participants reported significantly higher social presence, intention to use, and satisfaction for the extroverted NPC, suggesting that socially expressive personality cues can enhance users' perceived quality of interaction when the AI NPC serves as the primary social partner.

The gaze results further suggest that personality influenced different forms of visual attention. The extroverted NPC attracted greater total gaze duration and more frequent gazes, whereas the introverted NPC elicited longer average gaze duration per gaze. This pattern may reflect a distinction between \emph{social salience} and \emph{sustained processing}: extroverted cues may repeatedly attract users' attention, while the less expressive introverted NPC may lead to more sustained attention once users look toward it. However, these interpretations should be treated cautiously because gaze measures alone cannot establish the underlying cognitive process.

Importantly, the extroverted NPC did not elicit significantly more interaction turns, indicating that greater perceived social presence and visual attention do not necessarily translate into more active conversational behavior. Similarly, the similarity--attraction pattern was asymmetric: extroverted participants tended to prefer the personality-matched extroverted NPC, whereas introverted participants did not consistently prefer the introverted NPC. Thus, the findings suggest that the social appeal of extroverted cues may sometimes outweigh simple personality matching in individual human--AI interaction.

\section{Dyadic MR Studies}\label{sec:dyadic}

Building on the individual user study, the next stage examined whether the effects of AI NPC personality would persist or change when the interaction became socially shared. In many museum visits, users experience exhibits with companions rather than alone, which may redistribute attention and engagement between the AI NPC and the human partner. To address this context, we conducted a dyadic pilot study as an intermediate step toward a formal pair study. The pilot study explored how two co-located users interacted with each other and with the AI NPC in the MR museum, and helped identify interaction patterns, system issues, and study-design considerations for the subsequent formal study.

\subsection{Pilot Study}

The pilot study served as an exploratory bridge between the individual study and the formal dyadic study. Whereas the individual study examined one-to-one interaction between a user and an AI NPC, the pilot introduced a human partner into the MR museum experience to explore personality-driven AI NPCs in a shared interaction setting. It examined whether exhibit-specific AI NPCs could support dyadic interaction, how pairs coordinated their attention and communication during the shared experience, and what usability, personality manipulation, and task design issues should be refined before the formal study.

\subsubsection{Personality Design}

The pilot study retained the introversion--extraversion manipulation established in the individual study, including the use of LLM prompting and TTS-based cues. However, the NPC identities and exhibit contexts were adapted to the new museum setting and the transition from individual to dyadic interaction.

The original characters were replaced with two exhibit-specific historical figures: Emperor Huizong of Song represented the introverted condition, whereas Emperor Qianlong represented the extroverted condition. Each NPC was associated with a corresponding exhibit, with its appearance, historical identity, and dialogue content grounded in the surrounding physical and virtual artifacts. Although the characters and exhibit content changed, the underlying personality manipulation remained centered on the same introversion--extraversion contrast established in the individual study.

To accommodate dyadic interaction, the NPCs were configured to operate within a shared multi-user conversational context. Each interaction followed a one-request--one-response structure, such that the NPC generated a single response to the current user request. However, the full conversation history involving both participants was retained and provided as context for response generation. The system prompts also explicitly specified that two co-located users were jointly interacting with the NPC. This allowed the NPC to interpret each request in relation to the ongoing multi-party conversation while maintaining a consistent introverted or extroverted interaction style across turns.

\subsubsection{Collaborative Design}

Unlike the individual study, which used predefined questions to scaffold user-AI NPC interaction, the dyadic pilot study used an asymmetric clue-based task to elicit both human-human communication and human-AI NPC engagement. The task was structured around exhibit-related clues distributed asymmetrically between the two participants. Each participant received the same background story and task questions but was provided with different exhibit-related clues. This asymmetric information structure required participants to exchange information, discuss their interpretations, and coordinate their next steps in order to progress through the task.

To support this collaborative structure, the NPCs' task-related behavior was further constrained through system prompts. The NPCs were instructed to recognize the ongoing collaborative task, avoid directly revealing the answer, and provide indirect hints instead. They were also prompted to treat the interaction as a multi-user conversation and to consider the shared conversation history when responding.

\subsubsection{Participants and Procedure}

The pilot study was conducted at the Yang Ming Oceanic Culture \& Art Museum in Keelung, Taiwan. Six participants were involved, forming three acquainted pairs. Five participants were male and one was female. Participants were between 23 and 24 years old ($M=23.5$, $SD=0.5$). Based on self-reported trait extraversion, two participants were categorized as introverted and four as extroverted.

Each pair completed the collaborative MR task while interacting with both AI NPCs. Questionnaire responses, system interaction logs, gaze data, and observational notes were collected to assess the feasibility of the dyadic interaction design and to identify issues requiring refinement.

\subsubsection{Pilot Findings and Design Issues}

\paragraph{Personality Manipulation}

\begin{figure}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.5\linewidth]{figs/pilot_manipulation.png}

We assessed the personality manipulation at two levels: whether the intended personality distinction was represented in the configured personas and whether participants perceived the intended contrast during interaction.

At the persona level, direct assessment of the two configured NPCs yielded clearly differentiated Big Five profiles: the extroverted persona scored substantially higher on extraversion than the introverted persona (101 vs.\ 51), indicating that the intended personality contrast was represented in the persona configuration.

At the perception level, however, the intended contrast was not reproduced in participants' ratings. We calculated a net perceived extraversion score by subtracting perceived introversion from perceived extraversion ($\mathrm{PE}-\mathrm{PI}$), with higher values indicating stronger perceived extraversion. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:pilot_manipulation}, the NPC designed as extroverted received a lower mean net extraversion score than the NPC designed as introverted.

Together, these results suggested a discrepancy between the configured personality distinction and the personality cues perceived during live dyadic interaction. This discrepancy motivated refinement of how personality-related instructions were prioritized during response generation.

\paragraph{Task Difficulty}

Participants reported a low-to-moderate level of perceived task difficulty ($M=3.08$, $SD=0.66$, range $=2$--$4$ on a 7-point scale). The task was generally manageable while still requiring participants to interpret clues and coordinate with their partners. However, observational data indicated that the support provided by the NPCs was not always sufficient to help participants progress efficiently through the task.

\paragraph{Interaction and Usability Issues}

Observations during the pilot revealed several interaction issues. Some participants had difficulty reliably activating the virtual speech-input button, which occasionally disrupted attempts to initiate conversation with the NPC. Participants also experienced uncertainty regarding whether the system was available, listening, processing, or responding.

The NPCs' task-related guidance was also sometimes too indirect. Because the initial prompts strongly constrained the NPCs from revealing task answers, some responses provided insufficient scaffolding for participants to determine how to proceed. These observations suggested that both the interaction interface and the task-related prompting strategy required refinement before the formal dyadic study.

Based on the issues identified in the pilot, we refined both the system design and the study design before conducting the formal dyadic study. Table~\ref{tab:pilot_summary} summarizes the identified issues, the corresponding refinements, and the evidence used to assess the revised design in the subsequent study.

First, we expanded participant recruitment to include both acquainted and unacquainted pairs. Because the pilot included only acquainted pairs, pair acquaintance was held constant and could not be examined as part of the interaction context. Including both pair types in the formal study allowed acquaintance to be treated as an explicit between-pair factor and enabled us to examine whether responses to AI NPC personality differed across social contexts.

Second, we improved the speech-input interface and system-state feedback. The pilot revealed that participants sometimes had difficulty activating the virtual speech-input button and were uncertain about whether the system was available, listening, processing, or responding. The refined interface therefore explicitly indicated these four interaction states, allowing participants to better distinguish between an input that had not been registered and a response that was still being processed.

Third, we refined the personality prompts to strengthen the consistency of personality expression during interaction. Although the configured personas exhibited the intended personality distinction, the pilot manipulation check showed that this distinction was not consistently reflected in participants' perceptions. Rather than changing the target personality traits themselves, we modified the structure and priority of the personality instructions. Specifically, the personality-related instructions were moved to the beginning of the system prompt and explicitly marked as high priority so that they would take precedence when personality expression competed with task-related constraints during response generation.

Finally, we adjusted the task-related prompting strategy while preserving the collaborative structure of the asymmetric clue task. In the pilot, the restriction against revealing task answers sometimes resulted in guidance that was too indirect to help participants progress. The refined prompts therefore continued to prohibit the NPCs from directly providing the final answer but allowed them to offer more actionable scaffolding, such as presenting candidate options, narrowing the range of plausible interpretations, or directing participants' attention to relevant artifact features. This change was intended to support task progression without removing the need for participants to exchange information and collaboratively determine the answer.

The effectiveness of these refinements was subsequently assessed in the formal dyadic study. The revised personality manipulation was evaluated through participants' perceived introversion and extraversion ratings, task guidance was examined through post-study measures of task straightforwardness and mental effort, and interaction usability was assessed through ratings of interaction comfort, input reliability, and system predictability. The comparability of the acquainted and unacquainted groups was also examined before analyzing pair-acquaintance differences. These checks are reported together with the corresponding formal-study results below.

\subsection{Formal Dyadic Study}

Building on the personality-related findings from the individual study and the design refinements identified in the pilot study, we conducted a formal evaluation of the collaborative dyadic MR experience. The study examined how AI NPC personality shaped users' subjective evaluations and visual-attention behavior when interacting alongside another visitor, whether these responses differed between acquainted and unacquainted pairs, and whether user-NPC personality similarity showed different patterns across the two social contexts.

\subsubsection{Participants and Procedure}
For the formal study, we set up a designated experimental area within the Dah Hsian Seetoo Library in National Chengchi University in Taiwan. A total of 28 participants were recruited and assigned to 14 pairs: seven acquainted pairs and seven unacquainted pairs, yielding 14 participants in each acquaintance condition. The sample comprised 17 women and 11 men, with a mean age of 21.5 years ($SD = 1.69$, range 19-25). Participants' trait extraversion was assessed with the Extraversion domain of the BFI-2~\citep{sotoNextBigFive2017} on its native five-point scale, and averaged $3.03$ ($SD = 0.74$, range $1.45$-$4.55$). Exploratory checks were conducted to assess the comparability of the two groups. The results showed that the acquainted and unacquainted groups did not differ significantly in gender composition (Fisher's exact test, $p = 1.00$), age ($t = -0.55$, $p = .585$), or trait extraversion ($t = 0.74$, $p = .469$), indicating that the two conditions were demographically comparable.

After providing consent, participants completed a pre-study questionnaire that collected demographic information and the trait personality measure described above. They then underwent eye-tracking calibration on the Microsoft HoloLens 2. The researcher introduced the MR guide system, explained the collaborative task, and distributed the task materials. Each pair subsequently interacted with both AI NPCs, with the order of the introverted and extroverted conditions randomly assigned to pairs. During each condition, participants could explore the physical exhibits, interact with virtual artifacts, discuss clues with their partner, and ask the AI NPC for additional information. After completing both interactions, participants filled out a post-study questionnaire assessing their perceptions of the AI NPCs, social presence, and collaborative experience. Each MR interaction session lasted approximately 16 minutes, and the full procedure took approximately 35 to 40 minutes.

\subsubsection{Experimental Design}

The formal dyadic study employed a mixed design. AI NPC personality was manipulated within pairs, such that each pair interacted with both an introverted and an extroverted NPC, whereas pair acquaintance was treated as a between-pair factor contrasting acquainted and unacquainted pairs. This design allowed us to examine the effects of NPC personality on users' subjective evaluations and visual-attention behavior (RQ3), whether these responses differed by pair acquaintance (RQ4), and whether user--NPC personality similarity showed different patterns across the two acquaintance conditions (RQ5).

The study retained the personality design and collaborative interaction structure developed through the preceding studies, while incorporating the refinements identified from the pilot. The introversion--extraversion manipulation established in the individual study and adapted to a shared multi-user conversational context in the pilot was preserved. The historical NPC identities and exhibit-specific configuration introduced in the pilot were also retained, with Emperor Huizong of Song representing the introverted condition and Emperor Qianlong representing the extroverted condition. Each NPC continued to operate using a one-request--one-response structure while maintaining the full conversation history of both participants and a system-level specification that the interaction involved two co-located users.

The asymmetric collaborative task introduced in the pilot was likewise retained. Both participants received the same background story and task questions but different exhibit-related clues, requiring them to exchange information, discuss interpretations, and coordinate their next steps while interacting with the AI NPC within the shared human--human--AI setting.

The formal dyadic study incorporated the refinements identified through the pilot, including strengthened personality prompting, more actionable task scaffolding, clearer speech-input feedback, and the inclusion of both acquainted and unacquainted pairs (Table~\ref{tab:pilot_summary}).

\subsubsection{Measures}

\paragraph{Subjective Measures}

The post-study questionnaire was administered once for each AI NPC, using seven-point Likert scales throughout, with reverse-keyed items recoded. As a manipulation check, participants rated each NPC on perceived extraversion (``extroverted and energetic'') and perceived introversion (``quiet and reserved''), each measured with a single item; the net perceived extraversion score reported below is the difference between the two ($\mathrm{PE}-\mathrm{PI}$).

The focal outcome constructs were the same as in the individual study. Social presence was measured with five items adapted from ~\cite{gefenConsumerTrustB2C2004}, capturing a sense of human contact, individualized presence, social interactivity, warmth, and responsiveness. Continuance intention to use was measured with three items adapted from ~\cite{liChatbotsEcommerceEffect2023}, and satisfaction with three items adapted from Shi et al.

Beyond these constructs, the post-study questionnaire also collected measures of trust, perceived benefit, perceived co-participation, task straightforwardness, mental effort, predictability, naturalness of communication, and perceived personality match, together with a small number of open-ended questions. These measures are not analyzed here and are reported elsewhere.

\paragraph{Behavioral Measures}

Behavioral data were collected from system interaction logs and from the eye-tracking stream recorded by the MR headset. Interaction logs captured the full human-NPC dialogue, from which we derived the number of questions each participant directed to each NPC and the average length of those utterances. Because participants' speech was automatically transcribed, utterance length indexes how much a participant said rather than the sophistication of their phrasing.

Eye-tracking data were analyzed across the same three AOI categories used in the individual study: the AI NPCs, the physical exhibits, and the virtual exhibits. Two complementary measures were computed for each AOI. Gaze duration was accumulated from gaze-exit events, so that overlapping gaze events were not double-counted, whereas gaze count was computed as the number of fixation events. Because the two measures derive from different event types, they capture distinct aspects of visual attention: gaze duration reflects the overall allocation of attention, whereas the ratio of duration to count reflects how sustained each individual gaze was. Both are reported below.

\subsubsection{Manipulation Check}

Before addressing the main research questions, we examined whether the two AI NPCs were perceived as having the intended personality traits. Four planned comparisons were conducted using participants' perceived introversion (PI) and perceived extraversion (PE) ratings.

For the introverted NPC, perceived introversion was higher than perceived extraversion ($M_{\mathrm{PI}} = 4.46$ vs. $M_{\mathrm{PE}} = 3.61$), although this difference did not reach statistical significance (Wilcoxon $p = .143$). In contrast, the extroverted NPC was rated significantly higher in extraversion than introversion ($M_{\mathrm{PE}} = 4.82$ vs. $M_{\mathrm{PI}} = 3.36$, $p = .013$). Cross-NPC comparisons further showed that the extroverted NPC was perceived as significantly more extraverted than the introverted NPC ($4.82$ vs. $3.61$, $p = .033$), whereas the introverted NPC was perceived as significantly more introverted than the extroverted NPC ($4.46$ vs. $3.36$, $p = .050$). As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:pair_manipulation}, net extraversion scores ($\mathrm{PE}-\mathrm{PI}$) were generally negative for the introverted NPC and positive for the extroverted NPC.

Overall, three of the four comparisons were statistically significant and all four were in the intended direction, indicating that participants generally perceived the personality manipulation as intended. However, the distinction between introversion and extraversion within the introverted NPC was comparatively weaker.

To further examine whether pair acquaintance shaped responses to NPC personality, the dyadic sample was divided into acquainted and unacquainted pairs ($n=14$ participants in each group). As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:constructs_vs}, acquainted pairs showed little differentiation between the introverted and extroverted NPCs across social presence (SP), intention to use (ITU), and satisfaction (SAT). Mean difference scores were small across all three constructs, suggesting that acquainted pairs evaluated the two NPC personalities similarly (SP: $M_{\mathrm{diff}} =0.13$; ITU: $M_{\mathrm{diff}}=0.10$; SAT: $M_{\mathrm{diff}}=0.36$). Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (Table~\ref{tab:acquainted_construct_results}) indicated no significant differences for any construct (SP: $p=.649$; ITU: $p=.965$; SAT: $p=.875$), with correspondingly small effect sizes.

In contrast, unacquainted pairs showed a more consistent pattern of favorable evaluations toward the extroverted NPC. Positive mean differences were observed for SP ($M_{\mathrm{diff}}=0.50$), ITU ($M_{\mathrm{diff}}=0.62$), and SAT ($M_{\mathrm{diff}}=0.60$), indicating higher average ratings for the extroverted NPC across all three constructs. Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (Table~\ref{tab:unacquainted_construct_results}) indicated significant differences for ITU ($p=.041$, $r=.55$) and SAT ($p=.033$, $r=.57$), while SP showed only a marginal trend ($p=.050$, $r=.52$). Permutation tests showed a similar pattern, with significant differences for ITU ($p=.046$) and SAT ($p=.044$), whereas SP did not reach significance ($p=.096$). Taken together, these results suggest that the favorable evaluations of the extroverted NPC were more pronounced among unacquainted pairs, particularly for intention to use and satisfaction, while the evidence for social presence was less robust.

\subsubsection{Discussion}

The dyadic study suggests that the overall effect of AI NPC personality became weaker when another human partner was present. Although the extroverted NPC retained a descriptive advantage, the differences were smaller than in the single-user study. This pattern suggests that personality cues become less dominant when users must distribute their attention and engagement across the NPC, their partner, and the collaborative task.

Pair acquaintance further differentiated this effect. Acquainted pairs showed little difference between the two NPC personalities, whereas unacquainted pairs responded more favorably to the extroverted NPC, particularly in intention to use and satisfaction. One possible explanation is that acquainted pairs could rely on established rapport and coordination routines, whereas unacquainted pairs benefited more from the proactive and expressive behavior of the extroverted NPC. Personality effects may therefore depend on the broader social configuration rather than operating uniformly across dyads.

The gaze results nevertheless showed that personality-related attention differences remained present: the extroverted NPC attracted more frequent gaze, while the introverted NPC elicited longer average gaze duration. However, these attentional differences did not consistently translate into subjective evaluations. Similarity--attraction effects were also weak and inconsistent across acquaintance conditions. Together, these findings suggest that in collaborative MR interaction, effective support for the shared task and the existing relationship between users may be more consequential than personality matching alone.

\section{Cross-Study Results}

The cross-study analysis was conducted to examine whether the relative advantage of the extroverted NPC observed in the single-user study persisted across dyadic interaction contexts. Specifically, we compared three interaction conditions: single-user interaction, acquainted dyads, and unacquainted dyads.

\subsection{Subjective}\label{sec:cross-sub}

The analysis focused on the three subjective constructs that were measured consistently across the studies: social presence (SP), intention to use (ITU), and satisfaction (SAT).

\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{figs/output.png}
    \caption{Comparison of user ratings for introverted and extroverted AI NPCs across the single-user, acquainted-dyad, and unacquainted-dyad conditions.}
    \label{fig:boxplots}
\end{figure}

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:boxplots}, the extroverted NPC showed a larger descriptive advantage in the single-user condition, particularly for SP and ITU, whereas the differences between the two NPC personalities were generally smaller in the dyadic conditions. Because the single-user study employed a five-point Likert scale whereas the dyadic study employed a seven-point Likert scale, all questionnaire responses were first normalized to a common 0--1 scale before cross-study comparison.

To quantify the relative effect of NPC personality, a personality-difference score was calculated for each participant and construct as the normalized rating of the extroverted NPC minus that of the introverted NPC:

\[
D = Rating_{\mathrm{Extroverted}} - Rating_{\mathrm{Introverted}}.
\]

Positive values therefore indicated a relative advantage for the extroverted NPC, whereas negative values indicated a relative advantage for the introverted NPC. Kruskal--Wallis tests were then conducted to compare the distributions of these difference scores across the single-user, acquainted-dyad, and unacquainted-dyad conditions. To further characterize any cross-context differences, normalized ratings for the introverted and extroverted NPCs were also examined separately.

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:cross_study_kw}, the personality-difference score varied significantly across interaction contexts for SP, $H(2)=8.68$, $p=.013$. In contrast, no significant between-context differences were observed for ITU, $H(2)=2.51$, $p=.286$, or SAT, $H(2)=1.55$, $p=.461$. Thus, the clearest cross-study variation in the relative effect of NPC personality emerged for perceived social presence.

Because the omnibus test for $D_{\mathrm{SP}}$ was significant, pairwise Mann--Whitney $U$ tests with Holm correction were conducted as post-hoc comparisons. The SP difference score was significantly greater in the single-user condition than in both the acquainted-dyad condition ($U=176.5$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=.023$, $r_{\mathrm{rb}}=.576$) and the unacquainted-dyad condition ($U=167.5$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=.044$, $r_{\mathrm{rb}}=.496$). In contrast, the acquainted- and unacquainted-dyad conditions did not differ significantly ($U=92.5$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=.818$, $r_{\mathrm{rb}}=-.056$). These results indicate that the relative social-presence advantage of the extroverted NPC was significantly stronger in the single-user condition than in either dyadic condition, while the magnitude of this relative advantage did not differ reliably between acquainted and unacquainted dyads.

To further characterize this SP pattern, normalized ratings for the two NPC personalities were examined separately. SP ratings of the extroverted NPC differed significantly across interaction contexts, $H(2)=7.73$, $p=.021$, whereas SP ratings of the introverted NPC did not, $H(2)=2.25$, $p=.325$. Holm-adjusted post-hoc comparisons for the extroverted NPC showed that SP ratings were significantly higher in the single-user condition than in the unacquainted-dyad condition ($U=174.0$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=.032$, $r_{\mathrm{rb}}=.554$). The difference between the single-user and acquainted-dyad conditions did not reach statistical significance after correction ($U=163.0$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=.071$, $r_{\mathrm{rb}}=.455$), and no difference was observed between the acquainted- and unacquainted-dyad conditions ($U=104.0$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=.800$, $r_{\mathrm{rb}}=.061$).

No significant cross-context differences were found for ITU or SAT ratings of either NPC personality. Taken together, the cross-study results indicate that interaction context primarily affected the degree of differentiation in social presence between the two NPC personalities. The extroverted NPC exhibited a substantially larger relative SP advantage in individual interaction than in dyadic interaction, while the separate-condition analyses suggest that this pattern was associated more strongly with variation in evaluations of the extroverted NPC than with systematic changes in evaluations of the introverted NPC.

\subsection{Similarity Attraction}

To examine whether similarity--attraction effects varied across interaction contexts, the analysis used the normalized questionnaire ratings described in Section~\ref{sec:cross-sub}. Similarity was defined based on the experimentally assigned personalities of the AI NPCs. For each participant, a similarity score was calculated for the three constructs measured consistently across studies: social presence (SP), intention to use (ITU), and satisfaction (SAT). For extroverted participants, the score was calculated as the normalized rating of the extroverted NPC minus that of the introverted NPC; for introverted participants, it was calculated as the normalized rating of the introverted NPC minus that of the extroverted NPC. Positive values therefore indicated a relative preference for the personality-matched NPC, negative values indicated a relative preference for the personality-mismatched NPC, and values near zero indicated little or no similarity-based preference. This transformation aligned the direction of the similarity measure across participant personality groups, allowing similarity--attraction patterns to be compared across the single-user, acquainted-dyad, and unacquainted-dyad conditions.

Kruskal--Wallis tests first compared similarity scores across the three interaction contexts with introverted and extroverted participants pooled within each condition. No significant differences were found for SP ($H(2)=4.90$, $p=.086$), ITU ($H(2)=0.14$, $p=.930$), or SAT ($H(2)<0.01$, $p>.999$). However, descriptive patterns differed between introverted and extroverted participants, suggesting that pooling the two personality groups could obscure personality-specific variation. Comparisons were therefore conducted separately by participant personality.

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:cross_similarity_personality}, introverted participants exhibited a significant difference across contexts in SP similarity scores, $H(2)=8.93$, $p=.012$, $\epsilon^2=.385$. The median similarity score increased from $-.500$ in the single-user condition to $-.050$ in the acquainted-dyad condition and $.100$ in the unacquainted-dyad condition, descriptively indicating an attenuation of the pronounced preference for the personality-mismatched NPC observed during individual interaction. No significant differences were observed for ITU ($p=.563$) or SAT ($p=.729$). Among extroverted participants, SP similarity scores were positive in the single-user ($Mdn=.150$) and unacquainted-dyad ($Mdn=.133$) conditions but close to zero in the acquainted-dyad condition ($Mdn=-.017$). This difference did not reach statistical significance, $H(2)=5.81$, $p=.055$, $\epsilon^2=.212$. ITU ($p=.360$) and SAT ($p=.653$) likewise showed no significant differences across contexts.
